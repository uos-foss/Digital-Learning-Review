This portal helps Schools keep an eye on every
module's Blackboard course in terms of accessibility, reading lists, and alignment to the University's Blackboard template. 

### What this is

Rather than treating a VLE audit as a single once-a-year form to
fill in, the portal pulls together various data sources - Ally accessibility reports,
reading list status, whether the required template sections are in place -
so that problems can be spotted and supported early, not just recorded at the start
of the year.

It's a monitoring and support tool, not a scoring exercise aimed at
individuals. A finding on a module is a prompt to help, not a verdict on
whoever teaches it.

### Who it's for

* **Digital Learning Advisors (DLAs)**  work
  through the School Dashboard and and perform spot-checks - a manual look at a module - on around 30% of their Schools' modules.
* **School Leadership** use the School Dashboard to see how their whole
  school is doing - where things are in good shape, and where a module needs
  attention - without having to check each module by hand.
* **Faculty-wide leadership** use the Faculty Overview to compare across all
  schools at once.
* **Module Leads** can look up their own module on the **Module report**
  page to see its current accessibility, reading list and template status,
  and what's outstanding.

**A note for module leads:** if a module you lead comes up in a review, you
don't need to have filled anything in yourself. Audits are carried out by a
Digital Learning Advisor on your behalf, using what the portal's data shows
plus their own check of the module. Any finding raised with you comes from
that combination, not a personal judgement about your teaching.

### Where the information comes from

The portal brings together data the
university already holds, and is refreshed periodically:

* **SITS**, the university's student records system, supplies the module
  list itself.
* **Ally** checks how accessible course materials are for students using
  assistive technology (for example, whether a scanned PDF has readable text,
  or an image has an alt description).
* **Leganto** shows whether a module has a reading list, and whether it's
  published or still in draft.
* The faculty's **Template Alignment Report** shows whether
  sections of the Blackboard module template such as the a Welcome message, key staff
  contacts, assessment details etc. have been filled in and are visible to students.

Full details of each source, including how often it updates, are on the
**Help & Support** page under Resources & Support.

### Why a data-driven approach

The faculty has hundreds of modules and a small team of digital learning advisors. Checking
every module by hand, every year, in the same depth, simply isn't possible and it wouldn't be a good use of anyone's time to try, when most modules are
in reasonable shape most of the time.

Data like Ally and the Template Alignment Report are good
at telling you **whether something is there and whether it's been touched**:
is a reading list published, is the assessment section visible, does a file
have basic accessibility problems. What they can't tell you is whether the
content is actually good - accurate, current, well explained. That judgement
still needs a person.

So the portal uses the data to do the bulk of the routine checking at scale,
which frees up DLAs' time for the judgement calls that genuinely need a
human: looking closely at a sample of modules, and following up wherever the
data suggests something might be wrong.

### How spot-checking works

Alongside the automated signals from the data, Digital Learning Advisors choose a sample of
modules each semester to check by hand in depth - this is what "spot-check"
means in the portal.

Which modules get chosen is informed by:

* A DLAs own experience of the school and its modules,
* making sure the sample spreads across levels and courses rather than
  clustering in one place,
* some deliberate randomness, so coverage isn't predictable, and
* the same data indicators everyone else sees on the module list 
  accessibility scores, outstanding items, and template alignment - which
  can point an advisor toward a module worth a closer look.


### Your Module report

If you're a module lead, the **Module report** page is the one built for you.
Search for your module by code or name at the top, and you'll see:

* A summary line giving the module's level, a link to open the module site
  in Blackboard, and its **Audit Status**. Most modules show
  **Data-derived**, meaning the report below is built from the automated
  data alone. **Spot check-pending** means a Digital Learning Advisor has
  chosen this module for a closer look this year. **Spot checked** means
  that closer look has already happened.
* A short banner underneath summarising anything currently outstanding -
  reading list status, accessibility issues, template sections not yet
  visible - or a plain "nothing outstanding" line if there isn't anything.
* Two tabs below that:
  * **Module Checks and Readiness** - each section of the Blackboard module
    template (Welcome message, key staff contacts, assessment details, and
    so on) shown as a card, with whether it's visible to students and
    whether it's been worked on, plus a list of outstanding actions
    alongside.
  * **Accessibility Report** - the module's Ally scores, broken down by
    file and page content, and the specific issues behind them, ranked by
    severity, with links to guidance on fixing each type.

Everything on the page describes the module as at the latest data snapshot or
the most recent spot-check, not a mark or a grade for you personally - a card
showing a section still hidden is telling you what to do next, not recording
a failing.
