For module leads looking up their own modules on the Module report.

### What is this and who filled it in?

The Digital Learning Review is a faculty-wide picture of how modules are set up
in Blackboard. **The audit is carried out by Digital Learning Advisors on your
behalf.** You are not expected to fill anything in, and nothing here is asking
you to.

Most of what you see is not a person's opinion at all. It is drawn from the
University's own systems: the Blackboard Template Alignment Report, Ally,
Leganto and SITS. Where an advisor has reviewed a module themselves, their
finding is labelled as manually verified so it is never ambiguous which you are
looking at.

### What is it for?

Making sure students get a consistent, accessible experience across the
faculty: that they can find the module outline, know who to contact, see how
they will be assessed, and open the files you have given them.

It identifies what is outstanding. It does not fix it, and it is not a
judgement on your teaching.

### What am I looking at on the Module report page?

Search for your module by code or name at the top, and you'll see:

* A summary line giving the module's level, a link to open the module site in
  Blackboard, and its **Audit Status**.
* A short banner underneath summarising anything currently outstanding, such
  as reading list status, accessibility issues or template sections not yet
  visible, or a plain "nothing outstanding" line if there isn't anything.
* **Module Checks and Readiness**, a tab showing each section of the
  Blackboard template as a card, with whether it's visible to students and
  whether it's been worked on, plus a list of outstanding actions alongside.
* **Accessibility Report**, a tab with the module's Ally scores broken down by
  file and page content, and the specific issues behind them ranked by
  severity.

Everything describes the module as at the latest data snapshot or the most
recent spot-check. It is not a mark or a grade for you personally. A card
showing a section still hidden is telling you what to do next, not recording a
failing.

### There is a written comment on my module. Who wrote it?

A Digital Learning Advisor, when they audited the module. It appears under
Module Checks and Readiness as Additional Comments, and it is the one part of
the page written by a person rather than drawn from a system.

It is meant for you: usually what is outstanding and what would resolve it. It
stays as written until the advisor revises that audit, so it can describe
something you have since fixed. See "I have fixed the problem. Why does the
portal still show it?" below.

### What does "Data-derived" mean at the top of my module report?

It's the **Audit Status**, and it says how much human attention the module has
had. There are three:

* **Data-derived**: the report below is built from the automated data alone.
  Most modules show this.
* **Spot check-pending**: a Digital Learning Advisor has chosen this module
  for a closer look this year, and that hasn't happened yet.
* **Spot checked**: that closer look has already happened.

None of the three is a pass or a fail. They describe where the module is in
the review process, not how good it is.

### My module says a section is "Hidden from students". What does that mean?

That the section has been worked on but is still hidden, so no student can see
it. The work exists, it just isn't switched on. Making it visible in Blackboard
is all that is outstanding.

This is deliberately reported separately from "Not started", because the remedy
is one click rather than a piece of work.

### It says "Visible, unedited". I have definitely worked on that module.

That message is about one specific section, not the module as a whole, and it
means the report holds no record of that *section* being changed since the
course was created. Usually one of three things:

* The section still holds the template's placeholder text.
* The content lives somewhere else in the course, and that section was left as
  it came.
* You edited it very recently and the report predates your change. Check the
  import date in the sidebar.

It is flagged rather than failed: worth a look before treating it as done.

### Which sections am I expected to fill in?

Four of the fourteen template sections:

* **Welcome & Module Outline**
* **Key Staff Contacts**
* **Assessment Detail**
* **How Your Feedback Shapes this Module**

The first three ship **hidden** and need unhiding as well as writing. The
fourth ships visible but still needs somebody to actually write it, meaning how
this module's student feedback shaped it, so it counts as outstanding until it
has been edited.

The other ten are institutional boilerplate that arrive visible and correct.
Nobody expects you to touch them, and the portal does not count them against
you.

### My accessibility score is in the 90s but it still lists issues. Which is right?

Both. The score is weighted across everything in the course, so a handful of
genuinely inaccessible files can sit behind a very high overall percentage. A
single untagged PDF can be unusable for a screen-reader user while barely
moving a score built from hundreds of items.

Ally reports three figures and the portal shows all three, because they mean
different things:

* **Files**: uploaded documents such as PDFs, Word files and PowerPoints.
  Fixing these means correcting the source document and re-uploading it.
* **Editor pages**: pages built with the Blackboard content editor. These are
  usually fixable in place in a couple of minutes.
* **Overall**: the two combined, weighted by how much content sits behind each.

### How do I actually fix an accessibility issue?

In Blackboard, not here. This portal tells you what *kind* of thing is wrong and
why it matters. Your own **Ally Course Report** in Blackboard links every issue
straight to the file it is in, shows you the problem, and will often fix it in
place. Look for the Ally indicator icons beside your content and the
course-level Accessibility Report.

Deliberately, the portal does not give you click-by-click instructions, because
the exact menu path varies between Blackboard versions and a wrong instruction
would waste more of your time than no instruction.

### My reading list says Draft. What does that mean?

Leganto holds a list for the module but it has not been published, so students
cannot see it. **Missing** is different and means the module has been confirmed
as having no list at all. A blank status means the module was not in the export
either way, not that anything is wrong.

### I have fixed the problem. Why does the portal still show it?

Two possible reasons, and it is worth knowing which:

1. **No import since you fixed it.** The portal reads periodic exports, not
   live Blackboard. The sidebar shows when each source was last imported.
2. **An advisor has already audited the module.** Once a Digital Learning
   Advisor records a verdict on a template section, that verdict is what the
   portal uses from then on, and later imports do not change it. This stops the
   data contradicting somebody who actually went and looked, but it does mean a
   finding raised against you needs an advisor to revisit it, not just an
   import.

If you have acted on something and it is still showing well after the next
import, say so. That is the second case, and it needs a person.

### Can I see other people's modules?

You can see modules in your own school on the Module report. You cannot reach
the School Dashboard, the Faculty Overview or the Audit Portal.

### Something about my module is wrong, such as the lead name, or it isn't really running.

Both are fixable and neither needs a code change. Module leads are corrected in
the portal and survive future SITS imports. Modules that are not genuinely
running, such as skeleton shells, merged modules or archived records, can be
marked inactive so they drop out of every count. Report it through the **App
Feedback** tab on the Resources & Support page.
